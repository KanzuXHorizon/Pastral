# Code of conduct

## Our standard

Pastral contributors must maintain a professional, inclusive, evidence-driven environment.

Expected behavior:

- treat people with respect regardless of experience, identity, background, language, ability, or role;
- critique ideas, code, evidence, and outcomes rather than people;
- welcome questions and correct mistakes without ridicule;
- state uncertainty and distinguish measurements from assumptions;
- protect private/security reports and user data;
- give credit and respect licenses;
- accept reasonable review and change requests;
- prioritize user safety, accessibility, privacy, and project integrity.

Unacceptable behavior:

- harassment, threats, discrimination, sexualized conduct, insults, or sustained disruption;
- publishing private information or sensitive clipboard/security data;
- pressuring reporters to disclose vulnerabilities publicly before coordination;
- knowingly fabricating test, benchmark, compatibility, accessibility, or security evidence;
- retaliating against good-faith reports or reviews;
- using project spaces for spam, scams, malware, or unrelated promotion.

## Scope

This policy applies in repository discussions, reviews, issue trackers, private project channels, events, and public representation of the project.

## Reporting

For ordinary conduct concerns, contact the project owner privately through the established project channel. For security/privacy incidents, follow `SECURITY.md` and do not include sensitive material in a public issue.

Reports should describe the behavior, location/time, involved accounts, available evidence, and requested safety considerations. Do not investigate by accessing private systems or data without authorization.

## Enforcement

The project owner or appointed maintainers may:

1. provide a private correction or warning;
2. remove harmful content;
3. restrict participation temporarily;
4. remove repository/project access;
5. escalate credible threats or illegal activity to the appropriate platform or authority.

Decisions consider severity, intent, impact, recurrence, remediation, and user/project safety. Enforcement should be documented privately and avoid exposing reporters unnecessarily.

## Appeals

A person subject to enforcement may request private review by a maintainer not directly involved when one is available. An appeal does not authorize continued harmful behavior or disclosure of private reports.
